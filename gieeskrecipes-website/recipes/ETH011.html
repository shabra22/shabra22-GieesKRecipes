🫕 Ethiopian Cracked Wheat

ቅንጬ — Kinche

Ethiopian Kinche — cracked wheat or barley simmered until completely soft, drained, and dressed with kibbeh and a pinch of berbere or mitmita. The nourishing everyday porridge of Ethiopian households — quick, filling, and elevated by the addition of spiced clarified butter.

Kinche is the unpretentious daily porridge of Ethiopia — cracked wheat or barley, simply boiled until tender, then transformed by a spoonful of kibbeh. It is eaten for breakfast across the highlands, particularly during fasting periods when richer foods are not consumed.

Ingredients

Equipment: Medium saucepan

  • 1.5 cups cracked wheat (freekeh) or cracked barley
  • 4 cups water
  • 1 tsp salt

Dressing

  • 2 tbsp kibbeh
  • ½ tsp berbere or mitmita

Method

  1. Rinse cracked wheat or barley.
  2. Bring water to a boil. Add salt and grain.
  3. Simmer covered 20—25 minutes until completely tender.
  4. Most water should be absorbed. Drain any excess.
  5. Dish into bowls.
  6. Add a generous spoonful of kibbeh in the centre.
  7. Sprinkle berbere or mitmita over.

Chef Tips

  • Kinche should be tender but still have some texture — not mushy.
  • The kibbeh added at the end is what makes kinche kinche.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Over-cooking to mush.
  • Insufficient kibbeh.

Cultural Note

Kinche is the fasting food of Ethiopia's Orthodox Christian majority — during the 250+ annual fasting days, when animal products are avoided, kinche dressed with plant-based oil rather than kibbeh becomes the morning meal that sustains the faithful through the long days of prayer.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate 3 days. Reheat with a splash of water.

Reheating: Gently on stovetop.

Serve with: As a standalone breakfast, With ayib on the side, During fasting period as a fasting meal (without kibbeh)
