🌾 Ethiopian Roasted Barley Powder

ቤሶ — Besso

Ethiopian Besso — dry roasted barley grain ground to a coarse powder, mixed with a little water or honey into a paste or eaten dry. The ancient Ethiopian travel food and field snack — carried by farmers, soldiers, pilgrims, and traders for centuries. No cooking required on the journey.

Besso is one of Ethiopia's most ancient foods — essentially raw food technology, roasted and ground so it requires no cooking to eat. It is the predecessor of modern protein powders and energy bars, carried in small pouches and mixed with water or honey on demand. Nutritious, lightweight, long-lasting, and completely self-sufficient.

Ingredients

Equipment: Heavy dry pan, Grain mill, blender, or mortar and pestle

  • 2 cups barley grain — whole

To eat: water or honey to mix, and salt

    Method

    1. Heat a heavy dry pan over medium heat.
    2. Add barley grain. Toast, stirring constantly, for 15—18 minutes until deeply golden and fragrant.
    3. Cool completely.
    4. Grind to a coarse powder in a grain mill or blender.
    5. To eat: mix 2—3 tbsp besso with enough water or honey to form a thick paste.
    6. Season with salt.
    7. Or eat dry like granola.

    Chef Tips

    • Toast more deeply than you think — under-toasted besso tastes raw and starchy.
    • The grinding should be coarse — besso has texture.
    • On a journey: carry besso dry. Add water from any clean source.

    Common Mistakes to Avoid

    • Under-toasting.
    • Fine grinding — besso should be coarse.

    The Science

    See ETH018 for the science of roasted grain. Besso's pre-roasting converts the raw starch to partially gelatinised starch — making it more digestible without cooking and giving it a shelf life of months without preservation.

    Health Benefits

    • Very high in complex carbohydrate.
    • Barley beta-glucan.
    • Long-lasting energy.
    • No processing — completely whole grain.

    Cultural Note

    Besso is the food that Ethiopian Orthodox pilgrims carried on their long journeys to Lalibela, that farmers carried to the fields at dawn, that soldiers carried on campaigns across the highlands. It has sustained Ethiopian people for millennia without refrigeration, without cooking, and without anything except a handful of water.

    Storage & Serving

    Storage: Dry in an airtight container — indefinitely.

    Reheating: Not applicable.

    Serve with: On its own as a snack, Mixed with honey for breakfast, As a travel food
