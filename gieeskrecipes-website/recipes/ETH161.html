🥟 Ethiopian Fried Pastry

ሳምቡሳ — Sambusa

Ethiopian Sambusa — thin, crispy triangular pastry filled with spiced lentils, berbere, jalapeño, and coriander, sealed with a flour paste and deep-fried until deeply golden. Ethiopia's most beloved street food — the lentil filling has berbere and mitmita running through it, completely unlike Indian samosa.

Ethiopian sambusa is the street food that stops you in your tracks — the extraordinary crispiness of the pastry (much thinner and crispier than samosa), the intensely spiced lentil filling with berbere and jalapeño, and the dipping sauces (silsi or awaze) make it one of Africa's great street foods.

Heritage & Origin

Origin: All of Ethiopia — introduced via Arab and Somali trade routes, completely Ethiopianised

Sambusa (samosa in South Asian tradition) reached Ethiopia through Arab and Somali trading connections — but Ethiopian cooks have made it entirely their own. Ethiopian sambusa is made from a thin, crispy wheat pastry filled with spiced lentils or meat, sealed with a flour-water paste, and deep-fried until magnificently golden. During Ramadan, sambusa is the definitive iftar snack eaten after the daily fast — Ethiopian Muslim communities consume extraordinary quantities. It is also the most common Ethiopian street food, sold at every market, bus station, and street corner.

Ingredients

Equipment: Rolling pin, Deep frying pan, Thermometer

    Sambusa Pastry

    • 2 cups all-purpose flour
    • ½ tsp salt
    • ½ cup warm water
    • 2 tbsp oil

    Sealing Paste: 2 tbsp flour + 1 tbsp water

      Lentil Filling

      • 1 cup green lentils — cooked until very tender
      • 1 onion — very finely diced
      • 3 garlic cloves — minced
      • 1 green chilli — finely minced
      • 1 tsp berbere
      • ½ tsp cumin
      • ¼ cup fresh coriander
      • 1 tbsp oil
      • 1 tsp salt
      • Oil for deep frying

      Method

      1. // Make Filling
      2. Heat oil. Cook onion until golden. Add garlic, green chilli, berbere, cumin.
      3. Add cooked lentils. Mash roughly — some whole, some mashed. Mix well.
      4. Add coriander. Season. Cool completely.
      5. // Make Pastry
      6. Combine flour, salt, and oil. Add water gradually. Knead 5 minutes until smooth.
      7. Rest covered 20 minutes.
      8. Divide into 20 balls. Roll each very thin — 2mm, 12cm round.
      9. // Shape Sambusa
      10. Cut each round in half. Form a cone from one half.
      11. Fill cone with 1 heaped tsp lentil filling.
      12. Seal edge with flour-water paste. Press firmly.
      13. // Fry
      14. Heat oil to 175°C.
      15. Fry in batches 3—4 minutes until deep golden.
      16. Drain on paper towels.
      17. Serve immediately with silsi (ETH146) or awaze (ETH144).

      Chef Tips

      • The pastry must be rolled very thin — thick pastry produces a heavy sambusa.
      • Filling must be completely cool before filling — hot filling tears the pastry.
      • Seal with flour paste, not water alone — flour paste makes a firm bond.
      • The cone shape is Ethiopian — not all four corners folded.
      • 175°C oil — lower produces greasy sambusa; higher burns the pastry before the filling heats.

      Common Mistakes to Avoid

      • Thick pastry — heavy and doughy.
      • Warm filling — tears the pastry.
      • Insufficient sealing — filling escapes in the oil.
      • Wrong oil temperature.

      The Science

      The thin Ethiopian sambusa pastry (2mm) has a much higher surface-area-to-volume ratio than thicker samosa wrappers — this means Maillard browning occurs across a much larger proportion of the total mass, producing a significantly crispier result with less doughy interior. The flour-water paste seal undergoes gluten gelatinisation when fried, producing a stronger bond than plain water would.

      Cultural Note

      Ethiopian sambusa is the universal street food — found at every market, bus station, school gate, and mosque. During Ramadan, the smell of sambusa frying at iftar time is the smell of community — families and neighbours gathering at the end of the fast. The Ethiopian Muslim tradition of sambusa at iftar is one of the country's most beloved food rituals.

      Storage & Serving

      Storage: Best eaten immediately. Reheat in oven at 190°C for 8 minutes. Freeze uncooked 2 months.

      Reheating: Oven at 190°C — never microwave.
