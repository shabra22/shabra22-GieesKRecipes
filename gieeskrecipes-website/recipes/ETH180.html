🌽 Ethiopian Spiced Corn on the Cob

ቆሎ በቆሎ — Qolo Beqolo

Ethiopian Roasted Corn — fresh corn on the cob roasted directly over charcoal until the kernels char lightly, rubbed with lemon, salt, and mitmita. The universal African street food given its Ethiopian character by the mitmita rub.

Roasted corn is the most universal street food in sub-Saharan Africa, and Ethiopian roasted corn gets its distinctive character from the mitmita and lemon rub — the fierce heat of mitmita and the acidity of lemon transform it from ordinary to extraordinary.

Ingredients

Equipment: Charcoal grill or open flame

  • 4 fresh corn cobs — husks on

Mitmita-Lemon Rub

  • ½ tsp mitmita
  • 1 tsp salt
  • 2 tbsp lemon juice

Method

  1. Soak corn cobs in water 10 minutes.
  2. Place directly over charcoal, husks on.
  3. Roast 12—15 minutes, turning regularly.
  4. Remove husks when cool enough to handle.
  5. Mix mitmita and salt. Squeeze lemon over corn.
  6. Rub or sprinkle mitmita salt.
  7. Eat immediately.

Chef Tips

  • The husks char on the outside while steaming the corn inside — perfect cooking.
  • Apply mitmita and lemon while hot.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Not soaking — husks burn before corn cooks.

The Science

See TZA256 for corn roasting science. The Ethiopian mitmita rub combines capsaicin (which activates heat receptors) with the citric acid from lemon (which activates acid-sensing receptors) simultaneously — the combined sensory stimulation is more intense than either alone.

Cultural Note

Ethiopian roasted corn with mitmita is the harvest season street food that brings together the two elements that define Ethiopian street food — fire and fierce spice. The vendor's charcoal with corn cobs turning on it is the visual signature of Ethiopian market season.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Eat immediately.

Reheating: Not applicable.

Serve with: As a standalone street snack, At markets and festivals
