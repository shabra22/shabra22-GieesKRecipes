🐣 Ethiopian Easter Sweet (Fasika)

ፋሲካ ጣፋጭ — Fasika Tafach

Ethiopian Easter Sweet Bread — a small, individual-sized Difo Dabo (ETH004) made for Easter morning. Each person receives their own small bread loaf after the 55-day Lenten fast, marked with a cross and glazed with honey. The most emotionally significant sweet in Ethiopian Orthodox life.

The Fasika sweet bread is the most anticipated food in Ethiopian Orthodox Christianity — made in individual portions (one per family member), baked on Easter Saturday, and distributed after the midnight Easter service. After 55 days of fasting, each person holds their own small cross-marked honey bread.

Ingredients

Equipment: Individual muffin tins or shape by hand, Oven at 180°C

    Individual-sized Difo Dabo — make per ETH004 recipe

      Scale to make 16 small individual loaves (instead of one large)

        Each loaf: about 50g of dough

          Each marked with a deep cross

            Each glazed with Ethiopian honey immediately out of the oven

              Method

              1. Make Difo Dabo dough per ETH004.
              2. Divide into 16 equal pieces.
              3. Shape each into a small round loaf.
              4. Score a deep cross into the top of each.
              5. Place on a baking sheet.
              6. Rise 30 minutes.
              7. Bake at 180°C for 18—20 minutes.
              8. Immediately brush with warm honey.
              9. Serve to each person individually at the Easter feast.

              Chef Tips

              • Individual size is the point — each person receives their own.
              • The cross marking is sacred — deep and clear.
              • Honey glaze immediately out of the oven — while still hot.

              Common Mistakes to Avoid

              • Shallow cross.
              • Delayed honey glaze.

              Cultural Note

              The Fasika small bread is the most emotionally powerful food in Ethiopian Orthodox Christianity — the individual bread given to each person after 55 days of fasting, marked with the cross, glazed with honey. When the family gathers at Easter dawn and each person holds their own honey-glazed bread, the joy is not just about food — it is about endurance, faith, and community.
