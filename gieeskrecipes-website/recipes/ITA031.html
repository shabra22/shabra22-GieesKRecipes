🍝 Cannelloni

Cannelloni al Forno

Cannelloni are large pasta tubes stuffed with a savory meat or spinach-ricotta filling, blanketed in béchamel and tomato sauce, then baked until bubbling and golden.

Whether rolled from fresh pasta sheets or filled dried tubes, cannelloni is a special-occasion baked pasta dish, its name meaning 'large reeds' or 'canes' for the tube shape each piece is rolled or stuffed into.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large skillet, Mixing bowl, Piping bag or spoon, Baking dish

  • 12 fresh pasta sheets (or 12 dried cannelloni tubes)

Filling

  • 400 g (14 oz) ground beef
  • 1 small onion, finely chopped
  • 2 garlic cloves, minced
  • 250 g (1 cup) ricotta
  • 80 g (1 cup) grated Parmigiano-Reggiano
  • 1 egg
  • Salt and pepper, to taste

Sauce and topping

  • 500 g (18 oz) tomato passata or crushed tomatoes
  • 1 batch béchamel (as in ITA030, half quantity)
  • 80 g (1 cup) grated Parmigiano-Reggiano, for topping

Method

  1. Cook the ground beef with the onion and garlic in a skillet over medium heat until browned and cooked through, about 8–10 minutes. Season with salt and pepper, and let cool slightly.
  2. Mix the cooked beef with the ricotta, half the Parmigiano, and the egg until well combined.
  3. If using fresh pasta sheets, cut into rectangles about 10x15cm and blanch briefly in boiling water until just pliable, about 1 minute. If using dried tubes, no pre-cooking is needed.
  4. Fill a piping bag (or use a spoon) with the meat-ricotta mixture and pipe or stuff into the pasta tubes or roll into the fresh sheets.
  5. Preheat the oven to 190°C (375°F). Spread a thin layer of tomato sauce on the base of a baking dish.
  6. Arrange the filled cannelloni in a single layer over the sauce.
  7. Pour the remaining tomato sauce over the top, followed by the béchamel, and scatter with the remaining Parmigiano.
  8. Cover with foil and bake for 25 minutes, then uncover and bake a further 15–20 minutes until golden and bubbling.
  9. Rest for 10 minutes before serving.

Chef Tips

  • A piping bag makes filling the tubes far less messy and more even than a spoon.
  • If using dried tubes, make sure the sauce fully covers them so they have enough liquid to soften while baking.
  • Let the filling cool before stuffing — hot filling can tear delicate fresh pasta sheets.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Overfilling the tubes, causing them to split open during baking.
  • Not covering dried tubes adequately with sauce, leaving them hard and undercooked in spots.
  • Skipping the rest time, making the dish difficult to portion cleanly.

Health Benefits

  • A good source of protein and calcium.
  • Best enjoyed as a hearty main given its richness.

Cultural Note

Cannelloni is a popular special-occasion and Sunday lunch dish across Italy, valued for the elegant presentation of individually stuffed tubes compared to the layered simplicity of lasagna.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate for up to 3 days, or freeze baked or unbaked for up to 2 months.

Reheating: Reheat covered in a 180°C (350°F) oven for 20–25 minutes until warmed through.

Serve with: A simple green salad, Crusty bread, A medium-bodied red wine
