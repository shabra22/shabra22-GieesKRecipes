Pasta e Ceci

Pasta e Ceci swaps beans for chickpeas in this Roman cousin of pasta e fagioli — a rosemary-and-garlic-scented, deeply comforting soup thickened the same simple way, by mashing part of the chickpeas.

This is a dish of pure Roman resourcefulness, built from pantry staples — canned or dried chickpeas, a few aromatics, and small pasta — yet capable of tasting far richer than its humble ingredient list suggests.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large pot, Immersion blender or potato masher

  • 400 g (14 oz) canned chickpeas, drained and rinsed (or 250g dried, soaked and cooked)
  • 150 g (5 oz) small pasta (such as ditalini or broken spaghetti)
  • 60 ml (¼ cup) extra virgin olive oil, plus more for serving
  • 3 garlic cloves, minced
  • 1 sprig rosemary
  • 2 anchovy fillets (optional, for depth)
  • 2 tbsp tomato paste
  • 1 L (4 cups) vegetable stock
  • Salt and pepper, to taste
  • Red pepper flakes, to taste (optional)

Method

  1. Heat the olive oil in a large pot over medium heat. Add the garlic and rosemary, cooking gently until fragrant, about 1–2 minutes.
  2. If using, add the anchovies, stirring until they dissolve into the oil.
  3. Stir in the tomato paste and cook for 1 minute to deepen its flavor.
  4. Add the chickpeas and vegetable stock, and bring to a simmer.
  5. Remove about a third of the chickpeas and mash or puree, then stir back into the pot to thicken the soup naturally.
  6. Simmer for 15 minutes to let the flavors meld.
  7. Add the pasta directly to the pot and cook until al dente, adding more stock or water if needed.
  8. Remove the rosemary sprig, season with salt, pepper, and red pepper flakes if using.
  9. Serve hot with a generous drizzle of olive oil.

Chef Tips

  • The anchovies dissolve completely and add savory depth without any fishy taste — well worth including even for the hesitant.
  • Mashing part of the chickpeas is what gives this soup its characteristic creamy body without any dairy.
  • Add the pasta close to serving time so it doesn't overcook and turn mushy in the broth.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Skipping the tomato paste step, missing out on a layer of concentrated flavor.
  • Adding the pasta too early and letting the dish sit, resulting in bloated, overcooked pasta.
  • Using low-quality canned chickpeas, which can taste flat compared to well-cooked dried ones.

Health Benefits

  • Chickpeas are an excellent source of plant-based protein and fiber.
  • Naturally vegan and dairy-free, a nutritious and filling meal.

Cultural Note

Pasta e Ceci is a beloved Roman trattoria staple, often considered a close cousin to pasta e fagioli, with the chickpea version prized for its slightly nuttier, earthier flavor.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate for up to 4 days; add liquid when reheating as the pasta absorbs broth over time.

Reheating: Reheat gently on the stovetop, loosening with stock or water as needed.

Serve with: Crusty bread, A drizzle of good olive oil, A light red wine
