🍯 Struffoli

Struffoli

Tiny fried dough balls piled into a festive mound, bound together with warm honey and scattered with colorful candied sprinkles — a Neapolitan Christmas tradition.

Making struffoli is often a communal, multi-generational kitchen project in Naples around Christmas, with the tiny dough balls fried in batches and the final honey-bound mound assembled and decorated together as a family.

Ingredients

Equipment: Mixing bowl, Deep pot for frying, Large serving platter

  • 500 g (4 cups) 00 flour
  • 4 eggs
  • 2 tbsp granulated sugar
  • Zest of 1 lemon and 1 orange
  • 2 tbsp extra virgin olive oil or melted butter
  • Pinch of salt
  • Vegetable oil, for frying
  • 350 g (1 cup) honey
  • Colorful sprinkles (diavolina or nonpareils)
  • Candied fruit, chopped, for decoration

Method

  1. Combine the flour, eggs, sugar, citrus zest, oil, and salt into a smooth, firm dough. Rest, covered, for 20 minutes.
  2. Divide the dough into portions and roll each into thin ropes about 1cm thick.
  3. Cut the ropes into small pieces, about 1cm long, and roll each briefly between your palms into little balls.
  4. Heat vegetable oil to 170°C (340°F) and fry the dough balls in batches until golden all over, about 2–3 minutes per batch.
  5. Drain on paper towels and let cool slightly.
  6. Warm the honey gently in a large pan until it becomes fluid and fragrant.
  7. Add the fried dough balls to the warm honey and toss gently to coat evenly.
  8. Pile the honey-coated struffoli onto a serving platter in a mound or ring shape.
  9. Scatter with sprinkles and candied fruit while the honey is still warm and tacky, so the decorations adhere.

Chef Tips

  • Keep the dough balls genuinely small — much smaller than you might first think — as they puff slightly when fried.
  • Warm the honey gently rather than boiling it, which can make it too thick and difficult to toss the struffoli through.
  • This dessert is traditionally a shared, hands-on family project — enlist help rolling the many small pieces of dough.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Making the dough balls too large, resulting in a dense, less delicate finished texture.
  • Frying at too low a temperature, leaving the balls greasy rather than light and crisp.
  • Letting the honey cool too much before tossing, making it difficult to coat everything evenly.

Cultural Note

Struffoli is a beloved fixture of Neapolitan Christmas tables, its bright decorations and honeyed shine embodying the holiday's festive spirit throughout Campania.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Best eaten within 2 days; the fried dough softens over time once coated in honey.

Reheating: Not applicable; struffoli are served at room temperature.

Serve with: Espresso
