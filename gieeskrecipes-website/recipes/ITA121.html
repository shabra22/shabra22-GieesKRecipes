🍝 Pasta con le Sarde

Pasta chî Sardi

Sicily's celebrated sweet-and-savory pasta pairing fresh sardines with wild fennel, pine nuts, and raisins, a striking combination that speaks to the island's Arab culinary heritage.

The unexpected marriage of oily fish, sweet raisins, toasted pine nuts, and anise-scented wild fennel is thought to trace back to Arab rule in Sicily, and the dish remains one of the clearest surviving examples of that influence still cooked in Sicilian homes today.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large pot, Large sauté pan

  • 400 g (14 oz) bucatini or spaghetti
  • 500 g (1.1 lb) fresh sardines, cleaned and filleted
  • 1 large bulb wild fennel or fennel with fronds, chopped
  • 1 onion, finely chopped
  • 50 g (⅓ cup) raisins, soaked in warm water
  • 40 g (¼ cup) pine nuts, toasted
  • 4 anchovy fillets
  • Pinch of saffron threads
  • 80 ml (⅓ cup) extra virgin olive oil
  • 50 g (½ cup) breadcrumbs, toasted
  • Salt and pepper, to taste

Method

  1. Blanch the fennel in salted boiling water for 5 minutes, then remove with a slotted spoon, reserving the water for the pasta, and chop finely.
  2. Warm the olive oil in a large pan and sauté the onion until softened.
  3. Add the anchovy fillets and stir until they dissolve into the oil.
  4. Add the chopped fennel, drained raisins, and saffron, and cook for a few minutes to combine.
  5. Gently add the sardine fillets and cook briefly, just until they begin to flake, taking care not to break them apart too much.
  6. Bring the reserved fennel-infused water back to a boil and cook the pasta until al dente.
  7. Drain the pasta, reserving some cooking water, and toss gently into the sardine mixture with a splash of the water.
  8. Fold in the toasted pine nuts, season to taste, and plate.
  9. Finish each portion with a scattering of toasted breadcrumbs in place of cheese, which is never used with seafood pasta.

Chef Tips

  • Toasted breadcrumbs, called "poor man's cheese," are the traditional finishing touch instead of grated cheese.
  • Handle the sardines gently once added to the pan so they stay in recognizable pieces rather than disintegrating.
  • If wild fennel isn't available, cultivated fennel bulb with its fronds is a reasonable substitute.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Overcooking the sardines, which causes them to fall apart completely into the sauce.
  • Adding cheese out of habit, which is never used with this or any seafood pasta in Sicilian tradition.
  • Skipping the fennel-infused cooking water for the pasta, which adds a subtle but real layer of flavor.

Health Benefits

  • Sardines are rich in omega-3 fatty acids, calcium, and vitamin D.

Cultural Note

Pasta con le sarde is considered one of Sicily's defining dishes, its sweet-savory profile a direct culinary echo of centuries of Arab influence on the island.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Best eaten fresh; refrigerate leftovers for up to 1 day.

Reheating: Reheat gently with a splash of water, taking care not to overcook the fish further.

Serve with: A chilled Sicilian white wine such as Grillo
