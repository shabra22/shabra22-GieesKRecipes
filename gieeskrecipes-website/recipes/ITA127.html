🥨 Taralli Pugliesi

Taralli

Small, ring-shaped Puglian crackers, boiled briefly before baking like a mini bagel, resulting in a dense, satisfyingly crunchy snack often flavored with fennel seed or black pepper.

Taralli's distinctive crunch comes from the same boil-then-bake technique used for bagels and pretzels, and their long shelf life has made them a beloved everyday snack throughout southern Italy, equally at home with wine or on their own.

Ingredients

Equipment: Mixing bowl, Large pot for boiling, Baking sheet

  • 500 g (4 cups) 00 flour
  • 150 ml (⅔ cup) dry white wine
  • 120 ml (½ cup) extra virgin olive oil
  • 10 g (1¾ tsp) salt
  • 1 tbsp fennel seeds (or substitute black pepper)
  • 7 g (2¼ tsp) active dry yeast

Method

  1. Dissolve the yeast in a little of the wine, then combine with the flour, remaining wine, olive oil, salt, and fennel seeds into a firm, smooth dough.
  2. Knead for 8–10 minutes until elastic, then rest, covered, for 30 minutes.
  3. Pinch off small pieces of dough and roll each into a thin rope about 10cm long.
  4. Form each rope into a ring, pinching the ends together firmly to seal.
  5. Bring a large pot of water to a boil and drop the rings in a few at a time, boiling for about 1 minute until they float.
  6. Remove with a slotted spoon and drain on a clean towel.
  7. Arrange the boiled rings on a lined baking sheet and bake at 180°C (350°F) for 25–30 minutes until deeply golden and hard.
  8. Cool completely before serving — they continue to harden and crisp as they cool.

Chef Tips

  • Seal the ends of each ring firmly, or they may come apart during the boiling step.
  • The brief boil before baking is what gives taralli their characteristic dense, crunchy bite.
  • These keep exceptionally well in an airtight container, making them a great make-ahead snack.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Skipping the boiling step, resulting in a softer, more bread-like texture rather than the classic crunch.
  • Underbaking, leaving them chewy rather than properly crisp once cooled.
  • Not sealing the dough rings well, causing them to open up during boiling.

Health Benefits

  • A naturally vegan snack, though best enjoyed in moderation given the oil content.

Cultural Note

Taralli are ubiquitous throughout Puglia and much of southern Italy, sold in bakeries and enjoyed as an everyday snack, often flavored regionally with everything from fennel to chili to onion.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Store in an airtight container at room temperature for up to 1 month.

Reheating: Not applicable; these are meant to be eaten crisp at room temperature.

Serve with: A glass of wine, Cheese and cured meats as part of an antipasto spread
