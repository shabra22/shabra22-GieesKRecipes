🍬 Torrone

Torrone

A dense, chewy Italian nougat packed with toasted almonds, bound with honey and whipped egg white, traditionally cut into bars or bite-sized pieces around Christmas.

Making genuine torrone requires patience and a candy thermometer, slowly cooking honey and sugar to a precise temperature before beating it into whipped egg whites, resulting in a nougat with real chew rather than a brittle, hard candy.

Ingredients

Equipment: Candy thermometer, Stand mixer, Saucepan, Baking pan lined with rice paper

  • 300 g (1 cup) honey
  • 200 g (1 cup) granulated sugar
  • 80 ml (⅓ cup) water
  • 2 egg whites
  • 400 g (3 cups) whole almonds, toasted
  • Edible rice paper (wafer paper), for lining

Method

  1. Line a baking pan with edible rice paper.
  2. Warm the honey in a saucepan over low heat until it reaches about 130°C (265°F) on a candy thermometer, stirring occasionally.
  3. In a separate saucepan, combine the sugar and water and cook to the same temperature, about 130°C (265°F).
  4. Meanwhile, whip the egg whites to stiff peaks in a stand mixer.
  5. With the mixer running on low, slowly stream in the hot honey, then the hot sugar syrup, whipping continuously.
  6. Continue whipping on medium speed for 10–15 minutes until the mixture becomes very thick, glossy, and pale.
  7. Fold in the toasted almonds by hand, working quickly before the mixture sets.
  8. Pour into the lined pan, top with another sheet of rice paper, and press flat.
  9. Let set at room temperature for at least 4 hours, ideally overnight, before cutting into bars or pieces.

Chef Tips

  • A candy thermometer is essential for this recipe — precise temperatures are what determine the final texture.
  • Work quickly once the almonds are folded in, as the mixture firms up rapidly.
  • Edible rice paper prevents the sticky nougat from adhering to the pan and makes handling much easier.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Not reaching the correct temperatures for the honey and sugar syrup, resulting in torrone that's too soft or too hard.
  • Underwhipping the mixture, leaving a dense, gummy texture rather than the intended light chew.
  • Cutting before the torrone has fully set, causing it to lose its shape.

Cultural Note

Torrone has been made across Italy for centuries, with the towns of Cremona and Benevento both claiming particularly strong historical ties to the confection, especially around Christmas.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Store wrapped at room temperature for up to 1 month.

Reheating: Not applicable.

Serve with: Espresso, As part of a Christmas dessert spread
