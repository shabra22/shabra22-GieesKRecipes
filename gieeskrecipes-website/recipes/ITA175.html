🍖 Brasato al Barolo

Brasato al Barolo

A whole beef roast marinated overnight and then braised for hours in an entire bottle of Barolo, resulting in a dish so deeply flavored it's considered a pinnacle of Piedmontese celebration cooking.

Using an entire bottle of Piedmont's most prestigious wine is not an exaggeration but a genuine tradition, the long marinade and even longer braise transforming both the beef and the Barolo itself into a sauce of remarkable depth and silkiness.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large bowl for marinating, Heavy-bottomed pot with lid, Blender (optional)

  • 1.5 kg (3.3 lb) beef chuck roast, tied
  • 1 bottle (750ml) Barolo wine
  • 1 onion, chopped
  • 1 carrot, chopped
  • 1 celery stalk, chopped
  • 3 cloves garlic, crushed
  • 2 bay leaves
  • 2 sprigs rosemary
  • 4 juniper berries
  • Flour, for dredging
  • 80 ml (⅓ cup) extra virgin olive oil
  • Salt and pepper, to taste

Method

  1. Combine the beef with the wine, onion, carrot, celery, garlic, bay leaves, rosemary, and juniper berries in a large bowl or container.
  2. Cover and marinate in the refrigerator for at least 12 hours, ideally 24, turning occasionally.
  3. Remove the beef, pat dry, and strain the marinade, reserving both the liquid and the vegetables separately.
  4. Season the beef and dredge lightly in flour.
  5. Warm the olive oil in a heavy pot and brown the beef well on all sides, then remove.
  6. Add the reserved vegetables to the pot and sauté until softened.
  7. Return the beef to the pot and pour in the reserved wine marinade.
  8. Bring to a gentle simmer, cover, and cook very slowly for 2.5–3 hours, turning the meat occasionally, until fork-tender.
  9. Remove the beef and let rest. If desired, blend the remaining sauce and vegetables until smooth, or leave rustic.
  10. Slice the beef and serve with the rich Barolo sauce spooned generously over.

Chef Tips

  • The long marinade is essential, not optional — it's what allows the wine to penetrate and tenderize the meat over time.
  • Use a Barolo genuinely good enough to drink; the quality of the wine directly affects the finished sauce.
  • Blending the sauce at the end, if desired, results in an especially silky, restaurant-style finish.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Skipping or shortening the marinating time, missing much of the flavor development this dish depends on.
  • Using a low-quality wine, which won't reduce into a properly rich, balanced sauce.
  • Rushing the braise, leaving the meat tough rather than properly tender.

Cultural Note

Brasato al Barolo is considered a showcase dish of Piedmontese cuisine, traditionally reserved for special celebrations given the cost of using an entire bottle of prestigious Barolo.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate up to 4 days; flavor improves further the next day.

Reheating: Reheat gently on the stovetop or in the oven, covered.

Serve with: Creamy polenta, Mashed potatoes, The same Barolo wine used in cooking
