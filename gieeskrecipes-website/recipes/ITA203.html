🍪 Amaretti Morbidi

Amaretti Morbidi

Soft, chewy almond cookies with a cracked sugar-dusted top, made without any flour at all, their intensely almond flavor a beloved fixture of Italian coffee tables.

Unlike their crunchy cousins, morbidi amaretti are deliberately soft and moist inside, their naturally gluten-free almond-flour base making them a popular option for entertaining guests with dietary restrictions, without ever compromising on flavor.

Ingredients

Equipment: Mixing bowl, Baking sheet

  • 250 g (2½ cups) almond flour
  • 200 g (1 cup) granulated sugar
  • 2 egg whites
  • 1 tsp almond extract
  • Zest of 1 lemon
  • Powdered sugar, for coating

Method

  1. Whisk the egg whites until foamy but not stiff.
  2. Combine the almond flour and sugar, then fold in the egg whites, almond extract, and lemon zest into a soft, sticky dough.
  3. Rest the dough, covered, for 30 minutes to firm up slightly.
  4. Roll small portions into balls, about 3cm across.
  5. Roll each ball generously in powdered sugar to coat.
  6. Arrange on a lined baking sheet, spaced apart, and bake at 160C (325F) for 12-15 minutes until the tops crack and turn lightly golden, while staying soft inside.
  7. Cool completely on the tray before handling, as they are very delicate while warm.

Chef Tips

  • Do not overbake -- the goal is a golden, cracked exterior with a soft, chewy center, not a fully crisp cookie.
  • Rolling generously in powdered sugar before baking is what creates the characteristic cracked, snowy top.
  • These cookies keep well and often taste even better a day after baking.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Overbaking, which dries out the characteristic soft, chewy center.
  • Skimping on the powdered sugar coating, missing the traditional cracked appearance.
  • Handling the cookies while still hot, when they are fragile and can break apart.

Health Benefits

  • Almond flour provides protein, healthy fats, and vitamin E, and the cookies are naturally gluten-free.

Cultural Note

Amaretti are found in countless regional variations throughout Italy, with Saronno in Lombardy particularly famous for its version, a beloved accompaniment to coffee and dessert wines.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Store in an airtight container at room temperature for up to 1 week.

Reheating: Not applicable.

Serve with: Espresso, A glass of Vin Santo
