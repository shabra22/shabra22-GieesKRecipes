🥖 Cuddura cu l'Ova

Cuddura cu l'Ova

A sweet Calabrian Easter bread shaped into decorative rings, baskets, or braids, each traditionally cradling one or more whole, unpeeled eggs baked directly into the dough.

Like Casatiello further north, cuddura's whole baked-in eggs carry deep symbolic meaning for Easter, though this Calabrian version leans distinctly sweet rather than savory, and is often shaped playfully — dolls for girls, baskets for boys — for children in the family.

Ingredients

Equipment: Mixing bowl, Baking sheet

  • 500 g (4 cups) 00 flour
  • 10 g (3 tsp) active dry yeast
  • 150 ml (⅔ cup) warm milk
  • 100 g (½ cup) granulated sugar
  • 100 g (7 tbsp) unsalted butter, softened
  • 3 eggs, plus 4-6 whole raw eggs for baking into the dough
  • Zest of 1 lemon
  • Pinch of salt
  • Colorful sprinkles, for decorating

Method

  1. Dissolve the yeast in the warm milk with a pinch of the sugar and let sit for 5 minutes.
  2. Combine the flour, remaining sugar, salt, and lemon zest, then add the yeast mixture and 3 eggs, mixing into a dough.
  3. Gradually work in the softened butter until smooth and elastic.
  4. Let rise, covered, in a warm place for about 2 hours, until doubled.
  5. Divide the dough into portions and shape into rings, braids, or baskets as desired.
  6. Nestle whole, unpeeled raw eggs into each shape, securing with thin strips of reserved dough crossed over each egg.
  7. Let rise again for 30 minutes.
  8. Brush with a little beaten egg and scatter with colorful sprinkles.
  9. Bake at 180°C (350°F) for 20–25 minutes until golden.
  10. Cool before serving — the eggs cook through fully during baking.

Chef Tips

  • The whole eggs cook through completely as part of the baking process and are meant to be peeled and eaten with the bread.
  • Traditional shapes vary playfully — braided rings, baskets, or doll shapes — and are often made specifically for children.
  • Securing each egg with crossed dough strips keeps it in place through the final rise and bake.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Not securing the eggs well enough, causing them to shift during baking.
  • Overproofing the dough, which can cause it to lose shape when handled for the decorative forms.
  • Skipping the sprinkles, which are a traditional, festive part of the presentation for children.

Cultural Note

Cuddura cu l'ova is a beloved Calabrian Easter tradition, its playful shapes and baked-in eggs often made specifically to delight children during the holiday.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Store wrapped at room temperature for up to 3 days.

Reheating: Not applicable; served at room temperature.

Serve with: As part of an Easter breakfast or holiday table
