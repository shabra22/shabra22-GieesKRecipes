🥟 Pansotti con Salsa di Noci

Pansotti

Ligurian triangular ravioli filled with a blend of wild herbs and cheese, dressed in a rich, creamy walnut sauce rather than the region's famous basil pesto.

Pansotti's filling traditionally uses preboggion, a mix of foraged wild greens and herbs unique to Liguria, and the walnut sauce it's paired with is just as much a defining Genoese sauce as pesto, though far less internationally known.

Ingredients

Equipment: Mixing bowl, Pasta machine, Pastry cutter, Mortar and pestle or food processor

  • 400 g (3 cups) 00 flour
  • 3 eggs
  • Pinch of salt
  • 400 g (14 oz) mixed wild greens or a blend of spinach, chard, and herbs (parsley, marjoram), blanched
  • 250 g (1 cup) ricotta, well drained
  • 60 g (3/4 cup) grated Parmigiano-Reggiano
  • 1 egg, for the filling
  • 150 g (1 cup) walnuts
  • 1 clove garlic
  • 30 g (2 tbsp) unsalted butter, softened
  • 100 ml (scant 1/2 cup) heavy cream or milk
  • 40 g (1/2 cup) grated Parmigiano, for the sauce
  • Salt and pepper, to taste

Method

  1. Make the pasta dough with flour, eggs, and salt, kneading until smooth. Wrap and rest for 30 minutes.
  2. Squeeze the blanched greens thoroughly and chop finely.
  3. Combine the greens with the ricotta, Parmigiano, and egg into a smooth filling, seasoning well.
  4. For the walnut sauce, blend the walnuts, garlic, and a splash of water or milk into a coarse paste.
  5. Stir in the softened butter, cream, and Parmigiano until you have a thick, pourable sauce, seasoning to taste.
  6. Roll the pasta dough thin and cut into squares.
  7. Place small mounds of filling in the center of each square, fold into a triangle, and press the edges to seal.
  8. Bring a large pot of salted water to a boil and cook the pansotti for 3-4 minutes until they float.
  9. Drain and toss gently with the walnut sauce, thinning with a little pasta water if needed.
  10. Serve immediately.

Chef Tips

  • A mix of wild greens and herbs gives the most authentic flavor, though a blend of spinach, chard, and fresh herbs is a good approximation.
  • The walnut sauce should be thick but pourable - thin it with pasta water or a little extra cream as needed.
  • Seal the pasta triangles firmly to prevent the filling from leaking during cooking.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Using only spinach without any herbs, missing the aromatic complexity of traditional preboggion.
  • Making the walnut sauce too thick, leaving it difficult to toss through the pasta evenly.
  • Overfilling the pasta, making it difficult to seal properly.

Health Benefits

  • Walnuts provide healthy fats and vitamin E; greens contribute fiber and vitamins.

Cultural Note

Pansotti con salsa di noci is a defining Ligurian dish, its walnut sauce standing as a lesser-known but equally cherished counterpart to the region's famous basil pesto.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Fresh, uncooked pansotti can be frozen for up to 1 month.

Reheating: Best cooked fresh; do not reheat already-cooked pasta.

Serve with: A crisp Ligurian white wine
