🧇 Pizzelle

Pizzelle

Italy's oldest known cookie, thin, delicately patterned wafers pressed in a special iron and traditionally scented with anise or vanilla, said to date back thousands of years to ancient Abruzzo.

Pizzelle are pressed using an iron similar to a waffle maker, stamping each cookie with an intricate, often snowflake-like pattern, and the tradition is said by some to trace back to ancient Roman times, making them among the oldest cookies still made today.

Ingredients

Equipment: Pizzelle iron

  • 3 eggs
  • 150 g (3/4 cup) granulated sugar
  • 120 g (1/2 cup) unsalted butter, melted and cooled
  • 300 g (2 1/4 cups) 00 flour
  • 2 tsp baking powder
  • 1 tsp anise extract or vanilla extract
  • Pinch of salt
  • Powdered sugar, for dusting (optional)

Method

  1. Whisk the eggs and sugar together until pale and slightly thickened.
  2. Whisk in the melted butter and anise or vanilla extract.
  3. Combine the flour, baking powder, and salt, then fold into the wet ingredients to form a soft, sticky dough.
  4. Preheat the pizzelle iron according to the manufacturer instructions.
  5. Drop a small spoonful of batter onto the center of each mold and close the iron.
  6. Cook for about 30-60 seconds, or according to the iron guidance, until golden.
  7. Remove carefully and let cool on a wire rack - they crisp further as they cool.
  8. Dust with powdered sugar if desired before serving.

Chef Tips

  • Do not overfill the iron - a small spoonful is usually enough, as the batter spreads to fill the mold.
  • Pizzelle firm up and crisp as they cool, so do not worry if they seem slightly soft straight off the iron.
  • A specialized pizzelle iron with its distinctive patterned plates is genuinely needed for authentic results.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Overfilling the iron, causing excess batter to spill out the sides.
  • Removing the cookies too roughly while still very hot, which can cause them to tear.
  • Storing them before they have fully cooled and crisped, which can make them go soft.

Cultural Note

Pizzelle are considered among the oldest cookies still made in Italy today, with roots in Abruzzo tracing back centuries, and remain a fixture of Italian-American holiday baking traditions.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Store in an airtight container at room temperature for up to 2 weeks.

Reheating: Not applicable.

Serve with: Espresso, As part of a holiday cookie tray
