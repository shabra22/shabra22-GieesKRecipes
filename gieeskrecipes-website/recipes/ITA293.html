🍝 Maccheroni alla Mugnaia

Maccheroni alla Mugnaia

A thick, hand-pulled pasta from Scanno, traditionally rolled into one continuous strand around a wooden stick, tossed in a rich pork and tomato ragù thick enough to coat every ridge.

The technique behind maccheroni alla mugnaia is genuinely unusual — a single, very long strand of dough is rolled and pulled around a thin wooden rod, resulting in a rustic, chewy noodle designed specifically to trap a substantial meat ragù.

Ingredients

Equipment: Thin wooden dowel, Rolling pin, Heavy-bottomed pot, Large pot for pasta

  • 500 g (4 cups) 00 flour
  • 250 ml (1 cup) water, approximately
  • Pinch of salt
  • 600 g (1.3 lb) mixed pork (shoulder and sausage), cut into pieces
  • 1 onion, chopped
  • 1 carrot, chopped
  • 1 celery stalk, chopped
  • 150 ml (⅔ cup) dry red wine
  • 600 g (1.3 lb) canned crushed tomatoes
  • 80 ml (⅓ cup) extra virgin olive oil
  • Grated pecorino, for serving
  • Salt and pepper, to taste

Method

  1. Combine the flour, water, and salt into a very firm dough, kneading for 15 minutes until smooth. Rest, covered, for 30 minutes.
  2. For the ragù, warm the olive oil in a heavy pot and brown the pork well on all sides.
  3. Add the onion, carrot, and celery, sautéing until softened.
  4. Add the wine and let it reduce, then add the crushed tomatoes.
  5. Cover and simmer gently for 1.5–2 hours, until the pork is very tender, shredding it into the sauce toward the end.
  6. Roll the pasta dough into a large, thin sheet, then cut into one very long, wide strand.
  7. Wrap the strand around a thin wooden dowel and roll to thin and stretch it further, creating the traditional single long noodle.
  8. Bring a large pot of salted water to a boil and cook the pasta for 4–5 minutes until tender.
  9. Drain and toss with the pork ragù, serving with grated pecorino.

Chef Tips

  • The traditional technique produces one single, very long strand of pasta — a simpler cut into thick, long noodles works as a reasonable substitute.
  • The pork ragù needs genuine time to become properly tender and rich — don't rush this step.
  • This dish is traditionally associated with the mountain town of Scanno and its distinctive hand-pulled pasta technique.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Rushing the ragù, which leaves the pork tough rather than falling-apart tender.
  • Making the pasta too thin, losing the rustic, chewy bite that makes this pasta suited to a hearty sauce.
  • Skipping the shredding step, missing the way the ragù is meant to fully coat and cling to the thick noodles.

Cultural Note

Maccheroni alla mugnaia is a defining specialty of Scanno in Abruzzo, its unusual hand-pulled technique producing a single, very long strand of pasta traditionally rolled by local women.

Storage & Serving

Storage: The ragù keeps refrigerated for up to 3 days.

Reheating: Reheat the ragù gently on the stovetop; cook fresh pasta just before serving.

Serve with: A robust red wine from Abruzzo
