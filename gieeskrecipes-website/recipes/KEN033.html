🍠 Kenyan Sweet Potatoes

Viazi Vitamu

Viazi Vitamu — Kenyan sweet potatoes — boiled in their skins until perfectly tender and naturally sweet. One of Kenya's most beloved breakfast foods and afternoon snacks, served simply with tea or eaten on their own.

Sweet potatoes have been grown in Kenyan homesteads for generations, a reliable, affordable, and nourishing crop that serves both as breakfast and snack. Boiled in their skins and eaten with a cup of chai — especially Kenyan Masala Chai — they are one of the quiet pleasures of Kenyan domestic life. The orange-fleshed varieties are sweetest; purple-skinned varieties have a nuttier depth.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large pot with lid, Knife for testing

  • 4 medium sweet potatoes (about 800g) — scrubbed
  • 4 cups water
  • 1 tsp salt — optional

Method

  1. Scrub the sweet potatoes thoroughly under running water. Do not peel — the skins hold them together during cooking.
  2. Place whole in a large pot. Cover with cold water and add the salt if using.
  3. Bring to a boil, then reduce to a steady simmer.
  4. Cook for 25—35 minutes depending on size, until completely tender when pierced deeply with a knife.
  5. Drain and allow to cool slightly before handling.
  6. Serve whole — peel at the table and eat with tea or as a side dish.

Chef Tips

  • Do not overcook — the flesh should be tender but not falling apart.
  • Choose sweet potatoes of similar size for even cooking.
  • The natural sweetness needs nothing — serve plain or with a tiny pinch of salt.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Peeling before boiling — the skins protect the flesh and hold moisture in.
  • Overcooking until they fall apart.

The Science

Boiling sweet potatoes in their skins retains more nutrients than peeling first. The skin acts as a barrier, reducing leaching of water-soluble vitamins into the cooking water.

Health Benefits

  • Exceptionally rich in beta-carotene (vitamin A precursor) — supports vision and immunity.
  • Good source of vitamin C, potassium, and dietary fibre.
  • Low glycaemic index compared to regular potatoes.
  • Naturally sweet — satisfies sweet cravings healthily.

Cultural Note

Sweet potatoes are one of the most widely grown and consumed foods in Kenya, serving as a breakfast staple, afternoon snack, and weaning food for infants. Their natural sweetness and nutritional density make them a cherished part of Kenyan food culture across all communities.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate cooked sweet potatoes for up to 3 days.

Reheating: Microwave or reheat in a pan with a little water.

Serve with: Kenyan Masala Chai, Uji wa Wimbi, Boiled Arrowroots, Avocado
