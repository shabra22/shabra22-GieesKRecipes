🍛 Kenyan Goat Curry

Mchuzi wa Mbuzi wa Curry

Kenyan Goat Curry — bone-in goat pieces slowly simmered in a richly spiced tomato and onion sauce fragrant with curry powder, garam masala, turmeric, and ginger until the meat is falling off the bone. Bolder and richer than beef curry.

Goat curry is particularly popular in Kenyan coastal restaurants and Swahili households, where the natural richness of goat meat pairs beautifully with the aromatic spice blend. The longer cook time of goat compared to beef is an advantage here — the meat has more time to absorb the spices, and the sauce grows deeper and richer with every hour.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large heavy pot, Wooden spoon

  • 1 kg goat meat — bone-in pieces
  • 2 large onions — finely chopped
  • 4 medium tomatoes — diced
  • 5 garlic cloves — minced
  • 2 tbsp fresh ginger — grated
  • 2 tbsp vegetable oil
  • 1 tbsp tomato paste
  • 1½ cups water or stock
  • 2 tsp curry powder
  • 1 tsp ground turmeric
  • 1 tsp ground cumin
  • 1 tsp ground coriander
  • 1 tsp paprika
  • ½ tsp garam masala
  • 2 tsp salt
  • ¼ cup fresh coriander — chopped

Method

  1. Brown the seasoned goat pieces in hot oil in batches. Remove and set aside.
  2. Cook onions until deeply golden — 10 minutes.
  3. Add garlic and ginger. Cook 1 minute.
  4. Add tomatoes and tomato paste. Cook for 8—10 minutes until very thick.
  5. Add all spices except garam masala. Stir 1 minute to bloom.
  6. Return the goat. Add stock or water.
  7. Bring to a boil, reduce to very low heat, cover, and simmer for 75—90 minutes until the goat is completely tender.
  8. Stir in garam masala. Taste and adjust seasoning.
  9. Garnish with coriander and serve.

Chef Tips

  • Bone-in goat is essential for curry — the marrow enriches the sauce.
  • Garam masala added at the very end keeps its fragrance fresh.
  • A pressure cooker reduces cooking time to 35—40 minutes.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Using boneless goat — the sauce will lack body.
  • Adding garam masala at the start — it loses fragrance with long cooking.

The Science

Bone-in goat releases collagen and marrow into the curry during the long simmer, producing a naturally thick, rich sauce without any need for thickeners. This is why bone-in cuts are preferred for curry.

Health Benefits

  • Rich in protein and iron.

Cultural Note

Goat curry is a fixture of Kenyan coastal restaurants and Swahili households, reflecting centuries of Indian Ocean trade that brought Indian spices and cooking techniques to East Africa.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate for up to 4 days. Freeze for up to 3 months.

Reheating: Reheat gently on stovetop with a splash of water.

Serve with: Basmati Rice, Chapati, Naan, Kachumbari, Natural Yogurt
