🐟 Swahili Fish Stew

Mchuzi wa Samaki

Swahili Fish Stew — fresh fish fillets gently poached in a fragrant tomato and onion sauce spiced with turmeric, cumin, and coriander. The everyday coastal fish preparation — light, aromatic, and deeply satisfying with rice or ugali.

Mchuzi wa Samaki is the simplest and most versatile coastal fish dish — a quick tomato base elevated with Swahili spices and fresh fish poached gently within it. Where the full coastal experience of Samaki wa Kupaka requires grilling and coconut sauce, this is the weekday version — ready in 30 minutes and just as fragrant.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large wide pan with lid, Wooden spoon

  • 600g firm white fish fillets — tilapia, snapper, or sea bass
  • 2 medium onions — finely chopped
  • 4 medium tomatoes — diced
  • 3 garlic cloves — minced
  • 1 tbsp ginger — grated
  • 2 tbsp vegetable oil
  • 1 tbsp tomato paste
  • ½ cup water or fish stock
  • 1 tsp curry powder
  • 1 tsp turmeric
  • 1 tsp paprika
  • 1 tsp ground cumin
  • 2 tsp salt
  • ¼ cup fresh coriander — chopped
  • 1 tbsp lemon juice

Method

  1. Season fish with salt, turmeric, and paprika.
  2. Cook onions in oil until golden — 8 minutes.
  3. Add garlic and ginger. Cook 1 minute.
  4. Add tomatoes and tomato paste. Cook for 8 minutes until thick.
  5. Add curry powder, cumin, and remaining spices. Stir 30 seconds.
  6. Add water. Bring to a gentle simmer.
  7. Place fish in the sauce. Spoon sauce over fish gently.
  8. Cover and cook 8—12 minutes until fish flakes easily.
  9. Add lemon juice and coriander. Serve immediately.

Chef Tips

  • Do not stir after adding fish — it breaks apart.
  • Fish is done when it turns completely opaque.
  • Build the sauce fully before adding fish.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Stirring after adding fish.
  • Overcooking — fish becomes dry.

The Science

Fish proteins denature at 60—65°C — far lower than meat. Gentle simmering preserves moisture; vigorous boiling produces dry, rubbery fish.

Cultural Note

Mchuzi wa Samaki is the everyday fish dish of the Kenyan coast — quick, aromatic, and as natural to Swahili cooking as ugali is to the highlands.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Best eaten fresh. Refrigerate 2 days.

Reheating: Very gently over low heat.

Serve with: Wali wa Nazi, White Ugali, Chapati, Kachumbari
