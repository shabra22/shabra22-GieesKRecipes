🎂 Kenyan Coconut Cake

Keki ya Nazi

Kenyan Coconut Cake — a moist, tender cake made with coconut milk, desiccated coconut, and cardamom, baked until golden and fragrant. Kenyan Coconut Cake is the celebration cake of the Swahili coast — baked for Eid feasts, birthday gatherings, wedding receptions, and homecoming celebrations across coastal Kenya and beyond. The coconut milk in the batter keeps the crumb extraordinarily moist, the desiccated coconut adds texture throughout, and the cardamom gives it the distinctive aromatic character that makes it unmistakably Kenyan. This is recipe KEN150 — the 150th and final Kenyan recipe in the GieesK Recipes collection. A fitting finale.

Ingredients

Equipment: Two 20cm round cake tins or one 23cm tin, Stand or hand mixer, Mixing bowls, Wire rack

  • 2 cups all-purpose flour
  • 1½ tsp baking powder
  • ½ tsp bicarbonate of soda
  • ½ tsp salt
  • 1 tsp ground cardamom
  • 200g butter — softened
  • 1½ cups sugar
  • 3 large eggs
  • 400ml coconut milk
  • 1 tsp vanilla extract
  • 1 tsp coconut extract — optional
  • 1 cup desiccated coconut — plus extra for topping
  • 200ml coconut cream
  • 2 tbsp icing sugar
  • ½ tsp vanilla extract

Method

  1. Preheat oven to 180°C. Grease and line the cake tins.
  2. Sift together flour, baking powder, bicarbonate of soda, salt, and cardamom.
  3. Beat butter and sugar until pale and fluffy — about 5 minutes.
  4. Add eggs one at a time, beating well after each addition.
  5. Add vanilla and coconut extract if using.
  6. Alternate adding the flour mixture and coconut milk in three additions, beginning and ending with flour. Fold gently.
  7. Fold in desiccated coconut.
  8. Divide between tins. Bake for 35—40 minutes until a skewer inserted in the centre comes out clean.
  9. Cool in tins for 10 minutes, then turn onto a wire rack to cool completely.
  10. Whip coconut cream with icing sugar and vanilla until slightly thickened.
  11. Fill and frost the cooled cake with coconut cream frosting. Press extra desiccated coconut onto the sides and top.

Chef Tips

  • Room temperature butter, eggs, and coconut milk are essential — cold ingredients prevent proper emulsification.
  • Do not overmix after adding flour — overmixing develops gluten and makes the cake tough.
  • The cake is done when a skewer comes out clean and the top springs back when lightly pressed.
  • Toast the coconut for the topping in a dry pan for extra depth.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Cold butter — does not cream properly with sugar.
  • Overmixing after adding flour — tough, dense cake.
  • Under-baking — the centre remains gooey.

The Science

Coconut milk's fat content keeps the cake crumb moist by coating the starch and protein molecules, preventing them from bonding tightly during baking. This is why coconut milk cakes stay moister longer than cakes made with water. The desiccated coconut adds fibre that also retains moisture.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Room temperature 3 days unfrosted. Refrigerate frosted cake for up to 3 days.

Reheating: Not required — serve at room temperature.
