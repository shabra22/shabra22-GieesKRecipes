🥜 Kenyan Boiled Groundnuts

Karanga za Kuchemsha

Kenyan Boiled Groundnuts — raw groundnuts simmered in heavily salted water until tender and creamy. An entirely different eating experience from roasted groundnuts — soft, moist, and deeply savoury. A beloved Kenyan snack with its own dedicated following.

Boiled groundnuts divide opinion — you either love their soft, creamy, savoury character or you strongly prefer the crunch of the roasted variety. Those who love them are devoted. The shells become slightly soft during boiling, making them easy to crack open to reveal the plump, tender, seasoned nuts within. At coastal markets in particular, boiled groundnuts are a fixture.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large saucepan with lid

  • 500g raw groundnuts — in their shells
  • 2 tbsp salt
  • 4 cups water
  • Optional: 1 tsp chilli flakes, 2 garlic cloves — for flavoured version

Method

  1. Rinse the groundnuts in their shells thoroughly.
  2. Place in a large pot. Cover with water. Add salt generously.
  3. Add chilli and garlic if using.
  4. Bring to a boil. Reduce heat and simmer covered for 35—45 minutes.
  5. Test by opening one shell — the nuts inside should be tender and creamy, not crunchy.
  6. Drain.
  7. Serve hot or at room temperature, cracking shells to eat.

Chef Tips

  • Heavy salting of the water is essential — the salt must penetrate the shell to season the nut inside.
  • Test frequently after 35 minutes — the ideal texture is tender but not mushy.
  • Boiled groundnuts must be eaten within a day — they do not keep like roasted nuts.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Insufficient salt — bland nuts despite the long cooking time.
  • Overcooking — mushy texture.
  • Undercooking — still raw and crunchy inside.

The Science

Boiling groundnuts in their shells allows water and salt to penetrate through the permeable shell slowly. The starch in the raw nut gelatinises during boiling, converting the crunchy raw texture to a soft, starchy, bean-like consistency.

Health Benefits

  • Rich in plant protein.
  • Boiling preserves more water-soluble vitamins than roasting.
  • Good source of folate and minerals.

Cultural Note

Boiled groundnuts are a beloved alternative to the more common roasted variety — particularly popular at coastal markets and rural areas where they are often cooked in large pots and sold by the cupful.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Best eaten the same day. Refrigerate for up to 24 hours.

Reheating: Reheat in microwave or warm in a pan.

Serve with: Cold Soda, As a standalone snack, Alongside roasted maize
