🥪 Kenyan Club Sandwich

Club Sandwich ya Kenya

Kenyan Club Sandwich — toasted bread layered with grilled chicken, bacon, fried egg, kachumbari, sliced avocado, and garlic mayo. The Nairobi café favourite — the classic club given a Kenyan identity with avocado and kachumbari replacing plain lettuce and tomato.

The Kenyan club sandwich has evolved its own identity in Nairobi's thriving café scene — the addition of sliced avocado and a spoonful of kachumbari in place of plain lettuce and tomato gives it a tropical freshness that the original lacks. Served with masala fries, it is the definitive Nairobi café lunch.

Ingredients

Equipment: Toaster, Frying pan, Sharp knife

  • 6 slices bread — toasted
  • 200g chicken breast — grilled and sliced
  • 4 rashers bacon — grilled crispy
  • 2 eggs — fried
  • 1 ripe avocado — sliced
  • 3 tbsp kachumbari
  • 3 tbsp garlic mayo
  • 4 lettuce leaves
  • Salt and pepper

Method

  1. Toast all bread slices until golden.
  2. Season and grill chicken breast until cooked through. Slice thinly.
  3. Grill bacon until crispy. Fry eggs.
  4. Spread garlic mayo on one side of each toast slice.
  5. Layer 1: toast, lettuce, chicken, kachumbari.
  6. Layer 2: second toast, avocado slices, bacon, fried egg.
  7. Layer 3: final toast — mayo side down.
  8. Secure with cocktail sticks. Cut diagonally.
  9. Serve with masala fries.

Chef Tips

  • The kachumbari is the Kenyan difference — it adds a freshness and acidity that plain tomato cannot.
  • Avocado must be perfectly ripe.
  • Secure well with cocktail sticks before cutting — it will collapse otherwise.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Soggy bread — toast should be hot and crispy.
  • Cold ingredients — everything should be warm except the avocado.

The Science

The triple-decker structure allows the heavy, moist ingredients (egg, avocado) to sit in the middle layer, supported by toast above and below — preventing the structural collapse that a simple double sandwich would suffer.

Cultural Note

The Kenyan club sandwich represents the evolution of Nairobi's café culture — taking a universal format and giving it a distinctly local identity through the addition of avocado and kachumbari.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Best eaten immediately.

Reheating: Not recommended.
