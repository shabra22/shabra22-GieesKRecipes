🍪 Kenyan Coconut Cookies

Kuki za Nazi

Kenyan Coconut Cookies — crispy, golden cookies packed with desiccated coconut, fragrant with cardamom and vanilla. Inspired by the Swahili coastal love of coconut, adapted into a universally beloved baked cookie. The ideal accompaniment to Kenyan masala chai.

Kenyan coconut cookies combine the coastal love of coconut with the universal pleasure of a well-baked biscuit. The cardamom is the Kenyan signature — lifting the coconut into something more complex and fragrant than a plain coconut cookie. They are as at home with masala chai as they are with coffee.

Ingredients

Equipment: Baking tray, Mixing bowls, Oven

  • 2 cups all-purpose flour
  • 1 cup desiccated coconut
  • ½ cup sugar
  • ½ cup brown sugar
  • 1 tsp ground cardamom
  • ½ tsp ground cinnamon
  • ½ tsp baking powder
  • ½ tsp salt
  • ½ cup coconut oil — melted
  • 2 large eggs
  • 2 tsp vanilla extract

Method

  1. Preheat oven to 180°C. Line baking tray with parchment.
  2. Mix flour, coconut, both sugars, cardamom, cinnamon, baking powder, and salt.
  3. Combine coconut oil, eggs, and vanilla in a separate bowl.
  4. Mix wet into dry until a soft dough forms.
  5. Roll into walnut-sized balls. Place on tray with space between.
  6. Flatten slightly with a fork or palm.
  7. Bake 12—15 minutes until golden at the edges.
  8. Cool on tray for 5 minutes — they firm up as they cool.

Chef Tips

  • Do not over-bake — they should look slightly underdone in the centre when removed.
  • Coconut oil solidifies when chilled — work quickly if your kitchen is cold.
  • The cardamom is the Kenyan signature — do not reduce it.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Over-baking — very easy with coconut cookies.
  • Moving before cooled — they are fragile when hot.

The Science

Coconut oil has a different fatty acid profile from butter — its medium-chain triglycerides produce a crisper, less chewy cookie than butter-based versions. Combined with the natural fibre in the desiccated coconut, the result is a pleasantly crunchy texture.

Cultural Note

Kenyan coconut cookies have become a café staple — the biscuit that sits in glass jars on café counters across Nairobi and is eaten in the hundreds daily with tea and coffee.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Airtight container for up to 2 weeks.

Reheating: Not required.

Serve with: Kenyan Masala Chai (KEN131), Iced Kenyan Coffee (KEN249), As a standalone snack
