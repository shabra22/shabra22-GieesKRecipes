🥦 Kenyan Buttered Vegetables

Mboga za Siagi

Kenyan Buttered Vegetables — a colourful medley of seasonal vegetables blanched to perfect tenderness and finished in brown butter with garlic and fresh herbs. The restaurant vegetable side that proves vegetables deserve as much attention as the protein.

Hotel and restaurant buttered vegetables are dramatically better than boiled side vegetables because they are properly made — blanched to retain colour and texture, then finished in brown butter (beurre noisette) with garlic. The brown butter adds a nutty depth impossible to achieve with plain butter.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large pot for blanching, Bowl of ice water, Wide pan for finishing

  • 200g broccoli florets
  • 200g green beans — trimmed
  • 2 medium carrots — julienned
  • 1 yellow courgette — sliced
  • 100g baby corn

Brown Butter Finish

  • 3 tbsp butter
  • 2 garlic cloves — thinly sliced
  • 2 tbsp fresh parsley
  • 1 tbsp lemon juice
  • ½ tsp salt
  • ¼ tsp black pepper

Method

  1. Blanch each vegetable type separately in heavily salted boiling water — carrots 3 min, beans and broccoli 2 min, courgette and corn 1 min.
  2. Immediately transfer each to ice water to stop cooking and preserve colour.
  3. Drain and dry the blanched vegetables.
  4. Heat butter in a wide pan over medium heat until it begins to foam and turn golden-brown — the brown butter (beurre noisette).
  5. Add garlic — cook 30 seconds.
  6. Add all vegetables. Toss to coat.
  7. Add lemon juice, salt, and pepper.
  8. Toss with parsley. Serve immediately.

Chef Tips

  • The ice bath is essential — it stops cooking immediately and locks in the vivid colours.
  • Brown butter is the restaurant secret — nutty depth that plain butter lacks.
  • Watch the butter carefully when browning — it goes from brown to burnt in seconds.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • No ice bath — vegetables continue to cook and lose their colour and texture.
  • Burning the butter — bitter rather than nutty.

The Science

Blanching in heavily salted water (as salty as the sea) seasons the vegetables from within. The ice bath stops enzyme activity that breaks down chlorophyll — the bright green chlorophyll is preserved when cooking is stopped rapidly, but slowly degrades when vegetables are cooked past their prime or kept warm too long.

Cultural Note

Buttered vegetables at a Nairobi hotel are the side dish that separates a serious kitchen from a casual one — the technique required to preserve colour, texture, and flavour is a mark of culinary training and discipline.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Blanched vegetables keep 2 days. Reheat in butter.

Reheating: Toss in hot butter pan briefly.

Serve with: Every main course in this category, Alongside Mashed Potatoes (KEN277)
