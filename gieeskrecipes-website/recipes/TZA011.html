🥥 Tanzanian Cassava in Coconut Sauce

Muhogo wa Nazi

Muhogo wa Nazi — cassava simmered in fragrant coconut milk with garlic, turmeric, and coriander until each piece is infused with coastal sweetness and fragrance. The coastal Tanzanian cassava preparation — humble cassava transformed by coconut milk into something extraordinary.

Muhogo wa Nazi is the Tanzanian coast's most generous treatment of cassava — simmering it in coconut milk until each piece absorbs the sweetness and fragrance of the coconut. Sold at coastal markets and served as a snack throughout the day, it is one of the great simple pleasures of coastal Tanzania.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large pot

  • 600g fresh cassava — peeled and cut into chunks
  • 400ml thick coconut milk
  • 1 cup water
  • 2 garlic cloves — minced
  • ½ tsp turmeric
  • 1 tsp salt
  • 1 tsp sugar — optional
  • 2 tbsp fresh coriander

Method

  1. Peel cassava, remove woody fibre, cut into chunks.
  2. Pre-boil in salted water for 15 minutes until nearly tender. Drain.
  3. Combine coconut milk, water, garlic, turmeric, salt, and sugar in a pot.
  4. Add par-boiled cassava.
  5. Simmer gently for 10—15 minutes until cassava is completely tender and coconut sauce thickens.
  6. Scatter coriander. Serve.

Chef Tips

  • Pre-boiling gives better texture control.
  • Never boil coconut milk vigorously.
  • A pinch of sugar balances the coconut beautifully.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Boiling coconut milk hard.
  • Adding raw cassava directly — takes too long and over-reduces the coconut milk.

The Science

Pre-boiling controls cassava doneness before the coconut milk is introduced — allowing the final simmer to infuse flavour without risking the coconut milk over-reducing.

Cultural Note

Muhogo wa Nazi represents the Swahili coastal philosophy — the instinct to add coconut milk to anything and make it extraordinary. Cassava becomes a celebration food with this simple addition.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate 2 days.

Reheating: Gently on stovetop.

Serve with: Grilled Fish, Chai ya Tangawizi, As a standalone snack
