🫘 Tanzanian Rice with Beans

Wali wa Maharage

Wali wa Maharage — rice served alongside a thick, spiced bean stew. Tanzania's most complete affordable meal — the combination of rice and beans provides all essential amino acids in a single plate. Eaten daily by millions across the country.

Wali wa Maharage is Tanzania's most democratic meal — affordable, nutritious, filling, and universally beloved. The rice is always plain; the beans are cooked in a tomato-onion sauce until thick and creamy. Together they form a nutritionally complete meal that has sustained Tanzania for generations.

Ingredients

Equipment: Rice pot, Large bean pot

  • 2 cups long-grain rice
  • 3 cups water
  • 1 tsp salt

Bean Stew

  • 400g dried kidney or red beans — soaked overnight
  • 2 onions — diced
  • 3 tomatoes — diced
  • 3 garlic cloves — minced
  • 2 tbsp oil
  • 1 tsp curry powder
  • 2 tsp salt
  • ¼ cup coriander

Method

  1. Cook soaked beans in fresh water for 60—75 minutes until tender. Reserve liquid.
  2. Cook rice by absorption method.
  3. Cook onions until golden. Add garlic, tomatoes, and curry powder. Cook 8 minutes.
  4. Add beans and reserved liquid. Simmer 15 minutes until thick.
  5. Serve rice with bean stew alongside.

Chef Tips

  • The bean cooking liquid adds flavour — use it in the stew.
  • Beans should be completely tender.
  • The stew should coat the back of a spoon.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Under-cooking beans.
  • Discarding bean cooking liquid.

Cultural Note

Wali wa Maharage is Tanzania's most complete everyday meal — the combination that has fed the country's working people for generations. It appears at every mama lishe and is the default lunch for much of urban Tanzania.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate 4 days. Freeze 3 months.

Reheating: Gently on stovetop.

Serve with: Kachumbari, Pili Pili Sauce, Fried Plantain
