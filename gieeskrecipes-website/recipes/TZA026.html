🍛 Tanzanian Chicken Curry

Mchuzi wa Kuku

Tanzanian Mchuzi wa Kuku — chicken simmered in a rich tomato-coconut curry sauce fragrant with Tanzanian spices, fresh coriander, and green chilli. The everyday chicken curry of Tanzania — warming, aromatic, and deeply satisfying.

Tanzanian chicken curry is distinct from Indian curry — it is built on a tomato base rather than yogurt, uses coconut milk for richness, and the spicing is warmer than it is hot. It is the chicken dish that appears at every Tanzanian family meal, mama lishe stall, and celebration table.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large wide pan with lid

  • 1 kg chicken pieces — bone-in
  • 2 large onions — diced
  • 4 medium tomatoes — diced
  • 4 garlic cloves — minced
  • 1 tbsp ginger — grated
  • 200ml coconut milk
  • 3 tbsp vegetable oil
  • 2 tsp curry powder
  • 1 tsp turmeric
  • 1 tsp cumin
  • 1 tsp paprika
  • 1—2 green chillies
  • 2 tsp salt
  • ¼ cup fresh coriander

Method

  1. Brown chicken pieces in oil. Remove.
  2. Cook onions until golden.
  3. Add garlic, ginger, spices, and chillies. Cook 1 minute.
  4. Add tomatoes. Cook 8 minutes until thick.
  5. Return chicken. Add coconut milk.
  6. Simmer covered for 30—35 minutes until chicken is cooked through.
  7. Garnish with coriander.

Chef Tips

  • Bone-in chicken gives the best flavour.
  • Add coconut milk after the tomato base is well reduced — prevents splitting.
  • Never boil coconut milk hard.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Adding coconut milk too early.
  • Boneless chicken — less flavour.

The Science

Coconut milk contains proteins that stabilise at lower temperatures but break down above 82°C — never letting it reach a hard boil keeps the sauce smooth and emulsified.

Cultural Note

Chicken curry is Tanzania's most universally eaten meat dish — chicken is more affordable than goat or beef, and this fragrant curry makes it magnificent.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate 3 days. Freeze 3 months.

Reheating: Gently on stovetop.

Serve with: Wali wa Nazi (TZA002), Ugali (TZA001), Chapati (TZA004)
