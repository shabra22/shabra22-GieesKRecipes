🌿 Tanzanian Cassava Leaves

Kisamvu

Tanzanian Kisamvu — young cassava leaves pounded or finely chopped and slow-cooked with peanuts, coconut milk, and spices until completely tender and richly flavoured. One of Tanzania's most distinctive and nutritious vegetables — the dish that uses what most kitchens discard.

Kisamvu is one of the most nutritionally complete vegetable dishes in East Africa — the cassava leaves are high in protein, the peanuts add fat and more protein, and the coconut milk adds MCTs. Together they produce a dish of extraordinary nutritional density from ingredients that are entirely free or very inexpensive.

Heritage & Origin

Origin: Tanzania mainland and coastal regions — a uniquely East African vegetable preparation

Kisamvu (cassava leaves) is one of Tanzania's most distinctive vegetable dishes — the leaves of the cassava plant, which most cuisines discard, are harvested, pounded or finely chopped, and cooked until completely tender in a peanut and coconut sauce. Rich in protein and vitamins, kisamvu has sustained Tanzanian communities for centuries and represents the zero-waste food philosophy of the East African kitchen.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large pot, Mortar or food processor for leaves

  • 400g fresh young cassava leaves — washed and finely chopped or pounded
  • 3 tbsp groundnut paste (peanut butter)
  • 200ml coconut milk
  • 2 medium onions — diced
  • 3 tomatoes — diced
  • 3 garlic cloves — minced
  • 1 tbsp ginger
  • 2 tbsp oil
  • 1 tsp curry powder
  • 1 tsp turmeric
  • 2 tsp salt
  • 1 cup water

Method

  1. Pound leaves in a mortar or pulse in a food processor until very finely broken down.
  2. Blanch pounded leaves in boiling water for 10 minutes. Drain.
  3. Heat oil. Cook onions until golden.
  4. Add garlic, ginger, tomatoes, and spices. Cook 6 minutes.
  5. Add blanched leaves. Stir to combine.
  6. Mix groundnut paste with ½ cup warm water until smooth. Add to pot.
  7. Add coconut milk and remaining water.
  8. Simmer covered 30—35 minutes until completely tender and sauce is thick.

Chef Tips

  • Very young, tender leaves only — mature leaves are tough even after long cooking.
  • Pounding breaks down the fibrous leaf structure — essential for the right texture.
  • Blanching removes some of the naturally occurring compounds that require long cooking.
  • The groundnut paste must be mixed with warm water first — prevents lumping.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Mature leaves — too tough.
  • Not pounding — wrong texture.
  • Adding dry groundnut paste directly.

The Science

Cassava leaves contain linamarin (same as the root) which must be broken down through a combination of cell rupture (pounding) and heat (cooking). Pounding ruptures cell walls releasing the enzyme linamarase which breaks down linamarin; the subsequent blanching and long cooking ensures complete neutralisation.

Cultural Note

Kisamvu represents Tanzania's zero-waste food wisdom — the cassava leaf that would be discarded in most cuisines is transformed into one of the country's most nutritious and beloved vegetable dishes.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate 3 days.

Reheating: Gently on stovetop.

Serve with: Ugali (TZA001), Wali (TZA017), Fish or Meat stew
