Kaimati

Tanzanian Kaimati — soft, round wheat dough balls deep-fried until golden, then drizzled with a sugar syrup infused with cardamom and rose water. Zanzibar's celebration sweet — soft inside, crispy outside, fragrant with rose and cardamom.

Kaimati are one of Zanzibar's most beloved celebration sweets — soft dough balls fried until golden, then dipped while still hot into a rose water and cardamom sugar syrup that coats them in a fragrant, sticky glaze. They are made for Eid, for weddings, and for the most joyful occasions.

Ingredients

Equipment: Deep frying pan, Small saucepan for syrup

    Dough

    • 2 cups all-purpose flour
    • 1 tsp active dry yeast
    • 1 tbsp sugar
    • ½ tsp ground cardamom
    • Pinch of salt
    • 200ml warm water
    • Oil for deep frying

    Cardamom-Rose Syrup

    • 1 cup sugar
    • ½ cup water
    • ½ tsp ground cardamom
    • 1 tsp rose water

    Method

    1. Dissolve yeast and sugar in warm water. Rest 10 minutes until foamy.
    2. Mix flour, cardamom, and salt. Add yeast mixture.
    3. Mix to a soft, sticky batter — thicker than pancake, thinner than bread dough.
    4. Cover and rest 60 minutes until bubbled and doubled.
    5. Make syrup: boil sugar and water 5 minutes until slightly thickened. Add cardamom and rose water. Keep warm.
    6. Heat oil to 170°C.
    7. Drop tablespoons of batter into oil — they should be roughly round.
    8. Fry 3—4 minutes, turning, until deeply golden all over.
    9. Immediately dip hot kaimati in warm syrup. Toss to coat.
    10. Serve warm.

    Chef Tips

    • Dip in syrup while hot — the syrup penetrates while the kaimati is still steaming.
    • The batter should be thick enough to hold its shape but soft enough to drop from a spoon.
    • Rose water is the Zanzibar signature — do not omit.

    Common Mistakes to Avoid

    • Syrup too cold — does not penetrate.
    • Batter too thick — dense kaimati.

    The Science

    When hot kaimati are dipped in warm syrup, the differential in temperature creates a brief suction effect as the interior cools — drawing the syrup into the surface of the dough. This is why dipping while hot produces a more evenly glazed result than dipping cold.

    Cultural Note

    Kaimati are Zanzibar's Eid sweet — made in enormous quantities for the celebration, shared with neighbours and the needy, and eaten with chai in the morning after prayers. The smell of rose water and cardamom signals the Eid morning more powerfully than anything else.

    Storage & Serving

    Storage: Best eaten the same day.

    Reheating: Warm briefly in oven.

    Serve with: Chai ya Tangawizi, As a celebration sweet
