🥔 Tanzanian Potato Curry Fritters

Viazi Karai

Tanzanian Viazi Karai — boiled potatoes coated in a thick, heavily spiced chickpea flour batter and deep-fried until magnificently crispy. Served with coconut chutney and tamarind sauce. One of Zanzibar's most beloved street snacks — the Indian Ocean influence on the Tanzanian potato.

Viazi Karai (curry potatoes) is the Zanzibar street food that reflects the island's Indian Ocean trading heritage most directly — the bhaji technique applied to the boiled potato, producing a crispy, spice-crusted fritter that is simultaneously hearty and fragrant. Sold at every Zanzibar market.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large pot for boiling, Deep frying pan

  • 6 medium potatoes — boiled whole and cooled, halved

Spiced Chickpea Batter

  • 1 cup chickpea flour (besan)
  • ¼ cup water
  • 1 tsp curry powder
  • 1 tsp cumin
  • 1 tsp coriander
  • 1 tsp turmeric
  • 1 tsp paprika
  • 1 tsp chilli powder
  • 1 tsp salt
  • 2 tbsp fresh coriander — chopped
  • Oil for deep frying

Serve with: Chatni ya Nazi (TZA118) and tamarind sauce

    Method

    1. Boil whole potatoes in salted water until just tender — not falling apart. Cool completely. Halve.
    2. Make batter: whisk chickpea flour with water and all spices to a thick, smooth batter.
    3. Add fresh coriander to batter.
    4. Heat oil to 175°C.
    5. Dip each potato half in batter — coat thickly.
    6. Fry in batches 4—5 minutes until deeply golden and crispy.
    7. Drain on paper towels.
    8. Serve immediately with coconut chutney and tamarind sauce.

    Chef Tips

    • The batter must be thick — thicker than bhajia batter (TZA133). It should coat the potato heavily.
    • Completely cool potatoes before battering — warm potatoes slide off the batter.
    • The crust should be genuinely thick and crunchy.

    Common Mistakes to Avoid

    • Thin batter — falls off.
    • Warm potatoes — batter does not adhere.

    The Science

    See TZA133 for chickpea flour batter science. The thicker batter used for viazi karai has a higher protein-to-water ratio — when fried, the proteins form a denser, crunchier matrix that provides a more substantial crust than the thinner bhajia batter.

    Cultural Note

    Viazi Karai is the Zanzibar market snack that proves the Indian Ocean trade route in one bite — the South Asian bhaji technique applied to the Tanzanian potato by generations of coastal cooks until it became entirely its own thing.

    Storage & Serving

    Storage: Best eaten immediately.

    Reheating: Oven at 200°C briefly.

    Serve with: Chatni ya Nazi (TZA118), Tamarind Sauce, Pili Pili (TZA117)
