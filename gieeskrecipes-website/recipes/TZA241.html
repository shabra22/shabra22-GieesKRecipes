🍌 Chaga Banana Soup

Mbege wa Ndizi — Supu ya Chaga

Chaga Banana Soup — green cooking bananas boiled with kidney beans, sweet potato leaves, and a little salt in the traditional Chaga style. The foundational dish of the Kilimanjaro slopes — made from what the volcanic soil gives most abundantly.

Chaga banana soup is the dish that sustained the people who farmed Kilimanjaro's slopes for centuries — a complete, nutritious meal from green bananas (starch), kidney beans (protein), and greens (vitamins). Seasoned minimally because the ingredients are already full of flavour from the volcanic soil. No oil, no elaborate spice — just the mountain's gifts.

Heritage & Origin

Origin: Chaga people of the Kilimanjaro slopes — the banana farmers of Africa's highest mountain

The Chaga (Wachaga) are the Bantu people who farm the fertile volcanic slopes of Kilimanjaro — Africa's highest mountain. Their land produces some of Tanzania's finest bananas, and their cuisine is built almost entirely around the banana in every stage of ripeness: green for soup and stew, partially ripe for cooking, fully ripe for eating and fermenting. Banana soup (made from boiled green bananas with beans and greens) is one of the oldest Chaga preparations and reflects the remarkable nutritional wisdom of a people who built a complete diet around one crop.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large clay pot or heavy saucepan

  • 6 green cooking bananas — peeled and halved
  • 1 cup cooked kidney beans
  • 2 cups young banana leaves or sweet potato leaves (matembele)
  • 1 medium onion — roughly chopped
  • 3 cups water
  • 1.5 tsp salt

Method

  1. Peel green bananas wearing gloves — the latex sap stains.
  2. Combine bananas, onion, and water in a pot.
  3. Bring to a boil. Simmer covered 20 minutes until bananas are nearly tender.
  4. Add cooked kidney beans.
  5. Add washed and chopped greens on top.
  6. Cover and cook 8—10 more minutes until bananas are fully tender and greens wilted.
  7. Season with salt. Serve in the cooking liquid.

Chef Tips

  • Wear gloves to peel green bananas — the latex sap is very sticky and stains permanently.
  • The bananas should be soft but not falling apart.
  • The cooking liquid is as nourishing as the solids — eat as a soup.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Not wearing gloves.
  • Over-cooking — bananas become mushy.

The Science

Green bananas contain resistant starch — a type of starch that resists digestion in the small intestine and acts as a prebiotic, feeding beneficial gut bacteria. This is why traditional Chaga communities who ate green bananas daily had excellent digestive health.

Cultural Note

Chaga banana soup is the dish of Kilimanjaro — made from what the mountain gives, eaten in the shadow of Africa's highest peak. The Chaga Kihamba farming system, which produces these bananas in a sustainable multi-layer garden, has been recognised by the UN as a Globally Important Agricultural Heritage System.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate 3 days.

Reheating: Gently on stovetop.

Serve with: As a complete one-pot meal, With roasted meat on celebration days
