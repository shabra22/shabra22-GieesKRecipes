🍝 Zanzibar Seafood Linguine

Pasta ya Vyakula vya Bahari — Zanzibar

Zanzibar Seafood Linguine — linguine tossed with Zanzibar prawns, octopus, and clams in a coconut milk, tamarind, and Zanzibar spice sauce with fresh tomatoes and coriander. Italian pasta elevated by the full power of the Indian Ocean — Zanzibar's hotel restaurants at their finest.

Zanzibar seafood linguine is the dish that Zanzibar's finest hotels invented to bridge the preferences of international guests with the extraordinary quality of Indian Ocean seafood. The coconut-tamarind sauce is pure Zanzibar; the pasta is the universal medium that makes it accessible to everyone. The result is one of the most sophisticated dishes in Tanzania's modern culinary repertoire.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large pasta pot, Wide sauté pan

  • 400g dried linguine
  • 200g large prawns — peeled
  • 150g small octopus or squid — cleaned and sliced
  • 150g fresh clams or mussels

Zanzibar Coconut-Tamarind Sauce

  • 300ml coconut milk
  • 2 tbsp tamarind paste
  • 3 garlic cloves — thinly sliced
  • 2 shallots — diced
  • 3 medium tomatoes — diced
  • 2 tbsp olive oil
  • 1 tsp ground cumin
  • ½ tsp ground cardamom
  • ¼ tsp dried chilli flakes
  • 1 tsp salt
  • ¼ cup fresh coriander
  • 1 lime — zest and juice

Method

  1. Cook linguine in well-salted boiling water until al dente. Reserve 1 cup pasta water. Drain.
  2. Heat olive oil in a wide pan. Sauté shallots and garlic until soft — 3 minutes.
  3. Add cumin, cardamom, and chilli flakes. Stir 30 seconds.
  4. Add tomatoes and tamarind. Cook 5 minutes until reduced.
  5. Pour in coconut milk. Bring to a gentle simmer.
  6. Add clams or mussels first. Cover 2 minutes until they open.
  7. Add prawns. Cook 2 minutes until pink.
  8. Add octopus/squid last. Cook 90 seconds.
  9. Add drained linguine. Toss to coat. Add pasta water if needed.
  10. Finish with lime zest, lime juice, and coriander.

Chef Tips

  • Reserve pasta water — the starch thickens and binds the sauce beautifully.
  • Add seafood in order of cooking time — clams first, prawns second, squid last.
  • Al dente pasta is essential — it finishes cooking in the sauce.
  • The cardamom is the Zanzibar signature — do not omit.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Over-cooking seafood — becomes rubbery.
  • Not reserving pasta water.
  • Fully-cooked pasta before adding to sauce — becomes mushy.

The Science

Pasta water contains dissolved starch — when added to the sauce, this starch further thickens and emulsifies the coconut milk sauce, helping it coat the linguine uniformly. The final lime juice acid addition brightens all flavours and partially denatures surface proteins on the seafood, creating a subtle flavour enhancement.

Cultural Note

Zanzibar seafood linguine is what happens when the finest Italian pasta technique meets the Indian Ocean's greatest seafood and the Swahili spice tradition — a dish that could only exist in Zanzibar, that could only have been invented where these three food worlds collide.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Best eaten immediately.

Reheating: Very gently with a splash of coconut milk.

Serve with: Garlic bread, Simple green salad, Zanzibar white wine equivalent — cold ginger lemonade (TZA239)
